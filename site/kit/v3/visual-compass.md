# Odyssey — visual compass

The identity standard for Odyssey 3.0. Preserve the shared drawing and interaction language while giving each product a recognisable composition.

## One family, a real range

Odyssey combines warm paper, confident ink, vivid colour fields, rounded display character, crisp functional typography and illustrated everyday life. This edition must feel like an evolution of that family. Technical completeness cannot compensate for a generic appearance.

Expression changes composition and emphasis, not the meaning of controls or statuses. Expressive products use generous colour, lively narrative art and rounded display headlines. Composed banking uses a strong institutional colour field, large clear amounts, orderly rules and mature editorial art. Professionalism comes from hierarchy, precision and craft; it does not require an all-grey palette.

## Non-negotiable identity

- Colour has a job. Ink #07315F and paper #FAF9F5 anchor the core. Cyan #3BC1EE, coral #EB4E4D, mint #C9F3E8, lemon #F9E87A and lilac #DFD5FC are usable UI field colours as well as illustration colours. Pair bright fields with ink; full coral uses deep ink #071A2D for normal text. Keep error/success/pending tokens distinct from decorative colour.
- One dominant field. An expressive composition may add two supporting colour fields. A composed surface can use a saturated institutional field with one precise accent. Neither expression should become a wall of identical neutral cards or an indiscriminate rainbow.
- Type carries character. Baloo 2 Bold is the expressive English display voice; Hanken Grotesk carries functional text and composed English display. Beiruti carries Arabic with its own larger metrics. Numeric roles use Hanken and isolated LTR runs. Display character belongs in titles and editorial moments, not every label.
- Shapes form a vocabulary. Soft rectangular pockets and cards frame content; compact controls stay precise. Reserve pills for chips, badges and genuinely capsule-shaped controls. Do not turn every button, panel and navigation surface into the same pill.
- Composition creates authority. Use strong large/small contrast, purposeful alignment, readable data, clear separators and comfortable negative space. A bank does not become a new design system just because its brand colour differs.
- Illustration is a language. Share contour, palette logic and simplification across people, objects, environments and conceptual compositions. Vary subjects, viewpoints and silhouettes. A repeated welcome scene is not an illustration system.
- Motion has physical character. Use a short purposeful reveal or settle; preserve spatial continuity. Colour and type remain recognisable when reduced motion removes movement.

## Product identity survives the system

A product must remain recognisable through composition, type and navigation when its logo is covered. Respect original marks, source-backed typography and brand colours. Distinguish a balance-card stage, an open ledger and a bare balance with rules through structure, not recolouring. Core fonts and radii are defaults; verified institution identities can override them.

For a faceless illustration brief, keep the same contour, natural adult proportions, colour planes and narrative craft while omitting all facial features. Change category, viewpoint and silhouette between assets. Never make one dock or welcome scene every product's identity.

## The proof required

Build a compact set of system specimens showing expressive and composed treatments, with English/Arabic and light/dark checks. Demonstrate the same components, icon language and rules in both. Keep the compact system specimens as the shared reference. Product prototypes apply this reference; they do not imply runtime integration or exhaustive SDK parity.

All artwork comes from a dedicated image model or illustrator, with recorded prompts and provenance. Reference product names stay out of active Odyssey deliverables. Do not copy screenshots, draw substitute illustrations in code, or claim raster artwork is editable vector art.
