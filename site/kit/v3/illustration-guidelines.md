# Odyssey illustration language

Odyssey drawings bring observation, warmth and character to a useful product. Share the craft, not the composition. Artwork is commissioned from a dedicated image model or illustrator; designers and agents compose it with the UI. Code-drawn substitutes are not the production illustration method.

## The family resemblance

Use decisive ink-navy contours, simplified flat colour planes, coherent perspective, credible adult proportions and selective detail. Rounded joins and expressive gestures belong to the language; childish proportions and anonymous blob people do not. Treat hands, faces, object contact and architecture as observed forms. Avoid glossy 3D, photorealism, decorative noise and generic floating finance symbols.

The identity palette is ink #07315F, paper #FAF9F5, cyan #3BC1EE, coral #EB4E4D, mint #C9F3E8, lemon #F9E87A and lilac #DFD5FC. Skin/stone/object colours may use purposeful natural supporting tones. Choose a dominant field, then the supporting colours the story needs. Coral can be a major garment or object; it is not restricted to a tiny decorative dot. Colour intensity does not determine professionalism.

## Four categories, many compositions

| Category | Story examples | Composition options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human narrative | A shop exchange, a shared decision, a helpful conversation | Close crop, gesture-led group, observer viewpoint, asymmetric counter scene |
| Object study | Travel essentials, a carefully arranged workspace, a useful everyday tool | Still life, single object in detail, cutaway, deliberate overhead crop |
| Environment / architecture | A welcoming entrance, shaded street, quiet terminal or civic frontage | Frontal rhythm, corner perspective, elevated environment, cropped architectural detail |
| Conceptual editorial | Connection, preparation, progress, shared space | Interlocking physical forms, sequential objects, a visual metaphor grounded in real things |

Select the category before writing the subject brief. Adjacent editorial placements must differ in category, viewpoint or silhouette. A recoloured person-and-phone scene does not count as variety. A bank can use mature human narratives and architecture; it need not repeat shields, vaults, temples or money trees. Travel does not always need a plane, and mobility does not always need a car side view.

## Expression follows the task

**Expressive:** lively gestures, bolder crops, a vivid field, surprising but coherent object relationships. Character remains adult and credible.

**Composed:** strong proportion, precise contours, fewer competing focal points, carefully observed editorial subjects. Saturated navy or cyan can remain prominent. Authority comes from hierarchy and craft.

For confirmation, review, fees, balances and financial outcomes, facts remain dominant. Place editorial art outside the critical reading/action area. A drawing never implies a completed action that the product has not confirmed.

## Brief for an illustrator or image agent

```text
Asset ID and role: [stable name; orientation, education, discovery or editorial]
Audience and expression: [expressive or composed; explain the task]
Visual category: [human / object / environment / conceptual]
Story: [one meaningful sentence]
Composition: [viewpoint, crop, focal point, subject scale, aspect ratio]
Variation: [how this differs from adjacent assets in category/viewpoint/silhouette]
Shared craft: decisive rounded navy contour, controlled flat planes, coherent anatomy and perspective, selective detail, adult character.
Colour: [dominant field + supporting colours from the Odyssey palette; natural tones as needed]
UI placement: [intended pixel size; crop; clear separation from copy; no embedded text]
Reference: [approved craft reference, used for language rather than copying its scene]
Avoid: [subject-specific clichés or failure modes; no branding, watermark, malformed hands, glossy 3D or generic stock blobs]
Output: [PNG/WebP raster study or genuine editable illustrator source]; preserve prompt, references and approval status.
```

Do not freeze every illustration into one exact template. The invariant is the drawing language; the story, viewpoint, density and palette emphasis are deliberate choices.

## Review at delivery size

1. Read the scene at the actual UI size, then inspect anatomy/contact/perspective at full resolution.
2. Check silhouette, crop and composition against the adjacent artwork. Reject accidental repetition.
3. Check the relationship to the UI: colour hierarchy, legibility and adequate space for English and Arabic.
4. Keep scenes unmirrored unless their meaning requires a reviewed directional change. Never mirror numbers, marks, maps or QR codes.
5. Put text, statuses and actions in native UI. Decorative art uses empty alt text; meaningful art gets a short purpose-based description.
6. Keep vivid art in dark mode with an intentional light/coloured field; do not mechanically invert a raster.
7. Record internal review and user approval separately. Model colour drift or soft shading must be acknowledged; exact palette tokens belong to UI, not an unverified claim about every raster pixel.
